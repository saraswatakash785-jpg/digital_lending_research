# Evaluating the Impact of QR-Based Digital Lending on Micro-Retailer Growth

My B.Com (Honours) research project at Shaheed Bhagat Singh Evening College, University of Delhi, supervised by Ms. Seema Choudhary. I worked on it from November 2025 to May 2026 and presented it at two conferences in 2026.

The full paper is in this repo: 2287 Akash Saraswat Research (3).pdf.

## What I wanted to find out

Since COVID, most small shops in India take payments through QR codes. Fintech companies like Paytm, BharatPe and Mobikwik use that payment data to offer collateral-free loans, and they say these loans help small businesses grow. Most existing studies use company reports, so I wanted to go to the shops and ask the retailers myself.

Two questions guided the study:
1. Do QR-based fintech loans actually help micro-retailers grow their business?
2. Do retailers understand loan terms, repayment conditions and government credit schemes well enough to borrow wisely?

## How I did it

- **Primary data:** I surveyed 52 micro-retailers across different markets in Delhi (kirana stores, chemist shops, hardware stores and general shops that use QR payments). I used a structured questionnaire, mostly close-ended, and also talked to the shop owners in person.
- **Pilot:** I tested the questionnaire on about 15 retailers first and fixed unclear questions before the final survey.
- **Secondary data:** research papers, publications and industry reports.
- **Analysis:** I entered all 52 responses into Excel by hand, then used PivotTables and pivot charts to get frequencies, percentages and comparisons (descriptive statistics). I read the field conversations for recurring themes. I did not run significance tests, so the hypotheses are judged on descriptive evidence only.

## What I found

- 79% of the retailers had received a loan offer from a fintech company, but 81% had not accepted one.
- 69.23% said fintech interest rates are high.
- Other reasons for not borrowing: worry about repayment hitting daily cash flow, a preference for informal lenders they trust, and no real need for credit.
- All 52 retailers were unaware of the PM MUDRA Yojana.
- Most retailers did not report any clear improvement in their business after taking a fintech loan. Those who borrowed mostly used the money for short-term needs like stock or slow weeks, not for expansion. So the first hypothesis (loans improve business growth) was not supported.
- Financial literacy was low. Many retailers misunderstood loan terms, for example believing collateral or heavy paperwork is required. The second hypothesis (literacy affects understanding of loan terms) was supported.
- Some things I noticed in the field: the amount credited was lower than the sanctioned amount (one owner took Rs 2,00,000 and received Rs 1,93,000) while interest was charged on the full amount, and some POS devices were described as free but later had monthly charges deducted.

## Recommendations in the paper

Financial literacy campaigns with loan terms in local languages, simpler access to government schemes (including help desks in banks), and clearer disclosure of fees and disbursal amounts by fintech lenders.

## Limitations

The sample is small (52) and only from Delhi, so the results cannot be generalised to the whole country. I had no access to the companies' internal data. Some answers depend on what retailers remembered or were comfortable sharing about money.

## Tools

Microsoft Excel (manual data entry, PivotTables, pivot charts, descriptive statistics).
